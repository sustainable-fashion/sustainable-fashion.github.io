Circularity is the fashion industry’s favourite answer. But an answer to what, exactly? This one-day workshop puts the evidence, the design practice, and the emerging data infrastructure in the same room: what life cycle assessment shows, how circular collections are made and worn, and what the Digital Product Passport will demand of apparel.

Researchers, designers, students, and industry spend the day working it through together.

About

Closing a loop takes evidence, design, and data.

The day is built around three threads that rarely meet at the same table. Each is opened by people who work on it daily, and drawn back together by a closing panel.

Thread 01

The evidence

Life cycle assessment as a reality check: when a circular option genuinely lowers impact, when it merely moves it, and how to tell the difference before committing a collection to it.

Thread 02

The practice

Circular design from the studio and the wardrobe: multifunctional and zero-waste construction, longevity, heritage and vintage, and what a garment has to survive to stay in use.

Thread 03

The data

The Digital Product Passport moving from regulation to roll-out: standards, identifiers, information systems, and the first real apparel and accessory deployments now in the field.

Circular Lab

One day in a longer programme.

This workshop sits inside Circular Lab, the University of St.Gallen’s research programme for a sustainable circular economy in the Lake Constance region, and specifically inside its textiles focus area, where the work is on modular garment design, recyclability, and cutting the overproduction that accounts for a fifth of what the industry makes.

Circular Lab counters climate change and resource scarcity by advancing the circular economy: optimising business models, developing product designs, and building the ecosystems circular products need, across textiles and agri-food.

The programme

Six universities, thirty-plus companies

Funded with four million euros through Interreg Alpenrhein-Bodensee-Hochrhein and Switzerland’s New Regional Policy, Circular Lab works with partners across the DACH region on circular business models, ecosystems, and technologies.

Two focus areas

Textiles · Agri-food

The lab optimises business models, develops product designs, and helps build the ecosystems circular products need. This workshop belongs to the textiles strand; a parallel strand runs on agriculture and food.

The formats

Conferences, exchanges, workshops

Alongside the Circular Lab Conference, the CLab Exchange, webinars, and museum collaborations, days like this one are how the lab moves findings between research, companies, and the public.
